Pentridge Prison Tours opened B-Division and H-Division of Melbourne's former maximum-security bluestone prison to self-guided public tours for the National Trust of Australia (Victoria). The project won Best in Class for Architectural Design at the 2023 Australian Good Design Awards (from more than 900 entries) and took home the 2023 Victorian Premier's Design Award for Communication Design.

## Building inside strict heritage limits

Working under Heritage Victoria permits changes every practical assumption about museum AV. You cannot chase cables into 19th-century bluestone walls, drill casually into original cell doors, or clutter stark corridors with visible racks and touchscreens.

{{< figure src="/media/pentridge/pentridge-gatehouse.jpg" caption="Historic bluestone gatehouse at Pentridge in Coburg, Melbourne." >}}

I architected the site-wide Nodel show-control backbone tying together DMX and ArtNet theatrical lighting, BrightSign media playback, LiveSync projections inside cells, and audio cues triggered as visitors move through the divisions.

## Radios and televisions that belong in the room

Rather than dropping modern speakers into empty cells, we repurposed vintage radios and period televisions so archival voices and stories play out of hardware that actually looks like it belongs in the cellblock. Coordinating Nodel show control, low-profile networking, and ruggedised vintage enclosures kept the technology out of sight so the architecture and former inmates' voices carry the room.

<div class="qa-block">
	<h2>What do you actually do?</h2>
	<p>I sit between software engineering, physical hardware, and exhibition design. On any given week that might mean training a computer vision model on synthetic Blender renders, writing Python or Java control code, reviewing architectural conduit drawings with a builder, or sitting on a gallery floor tuning a projector warp at night before opening day.</p>
	<p>Right now I am Lead Creative Technologist at <a href="https://www.artprocessors.net/">Art Processors</a>, where I manage a team of four, run our prototyping lab (AP Lab), and lead technology delivery and post-opening support on permanent museum installations like <a href="/projects/phrontisterion/">Phrontisterion</a> at Mona.</p>
</div>

<div class="qa-block">
	<h2>How did you get into museum technology?</h2>
	<p>Through live audio and broadcast television. I studied AV at RMIT, worked on ABC TV sets and Melbourne music festivals, and won the SMPTE Young Industry Person of the Year award in 2013. Once I started my Software Engineering degree, I landed at Museums Victoria to help roll out <a href="/projects/nodel/">Nodel</a> across 427 exhibits. Staying close to physical rooms and real visitors stuck.</p>
</div>

<div class="qa-block">
	<h2>What kind of work do you care about most?</h2>
	<p>Technology that holds up five years after opening day and stays out of the way of the room. I like projects where high-precision engineering (computer vision, 250,000-step rotary encoders, 81-channel spatial audio, adaptive thermal models) disappears into something tactile and calm for the person standing in front of it.</p>
</div>
